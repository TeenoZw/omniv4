# Security Incident and Data Compromise Response Plan

## Purpose
To provide a repeatable response process for suspected or confirmed security incidents affecting personal information, systems, devices, or supporting records.

## Trigger events
- unauthorised access
- credential compromise
- loss of a laptop, device, or removable media
- suspicious administrative activity
- suspected database exposure
- unauthorised disclosure of client, driver, or location information

## Response steps
1. Detect and log the incident.
2. Contain the issue and preserve evidence.
3. Assess scope, affected systems, and affected information.
4. Escalate to the Information Officer and technical owner.
5. Record decisions, actions, and timeline.
6. Determine notification obligations.
7. Remediate and verify recovery.
8. Perform a post-incident review.

## Initial triage questions
- What happened and how was it detected?
- What systems, records, or users are affected?
- Does the incident involve personal information?
- Is the issue ongoing?
- What immediate containment action is required?
- Does access need to be revoked, tokens rotated, or passwords reset?

## Minimum evidence to preserve
- timestamps;
- relevant logs and screenshots;
- affected accounts, hubs, devices, or records;
- containment and remediation actions taken;
- who authorised the decisions; and
- notification decision and reasoning.

## Escalation and notification
The Information Officer should be involved where personal information may have been accessed, altered, lost, or exposed. The final notification decision should be documented even where the conclusion is that notification is not required.

## Minimum incident register fields
- incident reference
- date/time detected
- reporter
- systems affected
- categories of information affected
- containment actions
- impact assessment
- notification decision
- closure date
- lessons learned
